The directions a matrix doesn't rotate

Set a 2×2 matrix. Watch the unit circle deform into an ellipse, and see whether any directions survive unrotated — those are the eigenvectors. Drag the morph slider to watch the transform happen gradually, or drag inside the picture to move the test point.

Repeated eigenvalue, only one independent eigenvector — a shear. Not diagonalizable.
Complex eigenvalues — no real eigenvectors. Pure rotation (with scaling).
Drag inside the picture to move the test point

The dashed grey circle is the starting shape; the solid green curve is where the matrix sends it. Red and blue lines are the eigenvector directions — the only rays that map onto themselves, just longer or shorter by the eigenvalue. The orange point is a test vector: drag it and watch how far off its own ray the output lands, compared to how the eigenvector rays stay put.

Move the morph slider (t) to interpolate between the identity and the full matrix — eigenvector directions stay fixed the entire time, while every other point sweeps around them. That's the geometric meaning of "eigen."
