Visual guide — δ & α

Zoom into the cascade (r ≈ 2.9–3.6) and every period-doubling looks like a smaller copy of the last one. These two charts show why — the spacing between bifurcations and the width of each branch both shrink by a fixed factor, step after step.

1 · Spacing shrinks by δ
Each gap Δrn between one period-doubling and the next is about 1/4.669 the size of the gap before it.
—
2 · Branch width shrinks by α
At each superstable rn, the branch width dn = f2ⁿ⁻¹(½)−½ shrinks by about 1/2.503 from one level to the next.
—

Neither ratio is put in by hand — both come out of iterating xn+1 = r·xn(1−xn) and locating where the critical point x = 0.5 lands exactly back on the cycle. Because the ratios settle to fixed numbers (δ ≈ 4.6692, α ≈ 2.5029), the picture keeps repeating itself at every scale — that's the self-similarity you see when you drag-zoom anywhere in the cascade.

Feigenbaum constants — derived, not quoted

δ and α are not fitted to this picture — they fall out of the period-doubling cascade itself. Every 2n-cycle has a superstable parameter rn, the value of r at which the critical point x = 0.5 lands exactly on the cycle (where the cycle's multiplier is zero). Locating each rn numerically and taking limiting ratios recovers both constants directly from their definitions.

δ = limn→∞  (rn−1 − rn−2) / (rn − rn−1) — ratio of successive spacings between bifurcations
α = limn→∞  dn / dn+1 — ratio of branch width to sub-branch width, where dn = f2ⁿ⁻¹(½; rn) − ½
