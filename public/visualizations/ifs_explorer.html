Attractors from Contraction & Rotation

Pick $n$ anchor points $p_0,\dots,p_{n-1}$. Each map $A_i(x) = p_i + r\,R_\theta(x-p_i)$ spins by $\theta$ and shrinks by $r$ around its own anchor. Play the chaos game — jump to a random anchor's map over and over — and the points settle onto the one attractor these maps share.

What you're looking at

θ = 0° turns this into the Sierpinski chaos game — plain contraction toward each anchor, no spin. Turning θ up bends the straight lines toward each vertex into curling, branching arms instead.

r controls how much each step shrinks: closer to 0.5 (matching the book's example) gives self-similar copies at every scale; push r toward 0.9 and the arms overlap into denser, bushier shapes.
